## What changed since `d0f1ac30`

The holistic architecture is preserved: all-domain assessment, complementary selection, budgets, restraint, and Confidence/strategy independence. This iteration changes realization and one policy authority.

1. **Shared section contracts** (`shared/BriefingSectionContracts.js`). Every briefing type now declares its sections, and each section has one job. Each section consumes a *facet* of the same synthesis:

   | Section | Facet | Job |
   |---|---|---|
   | headline | character | short, no numbers |
   | recap | evidence | |
   | meaning | goal implication | |
   | confidence | outlook | |
   | takeaway | interpretation | adds no new numbers or insights |
   | coaching | execution | |
   | action | commitment | |
   | watch | discriminator | |

   Budgets differ by type:
   - **Midweek:** 4 sections and the least copy.
   - **Weekly:** 8 sections, moderate.
   - **Monthly:** more evidence per section.
   - **DEXA:** outcome-led.
   - **Photo:** visual-led, gaining interpretation and execution sections only when the photos show strong change.
2. **Anti-redundancy that goes beyond string equality.**
   - **Structural audit:**
     - no insight is reused in the same capacity;
     - the takeaway adds nothing the recap did not tell;
     - sections use only what synthesis considered.
   - **Text audit:**
     - the headline stays within budget and states no numbers;
     - each specific quantity is stated in one section only;
     - the takeaway states no quantity;
     - no section pair that must not paraphrase each other mostly shares its content words;
     - the action commits to something and the watch looks forward;
     - the whole Weekly stays within its word budget.
3. **Weekly realization.**
   - The hero headline is now a short synthesis of the week.
   - The evidence sentence moved into the hero paragraph.
   - Biggest Takeaway is the coach's read: what is working and what matters now.
   - What To Do carries execution: a supporting example, the second-tier finding, and what to do about unreadable logs.
   - Into Next Week is the commitment, and What To Watch covers what decides next week.
   - Headline, paragraph and takeaway are generated from the same lead insights, and are no longer the same sentence.
4. **Weight-pace authority (§6).** The generic lb/week thresholds are gone. Goal types own direction only.
   - The scale's pace can be called fast or on-pace only against a canonical expected range on the accepted Phase Expected Trajectory. Today's records declare none.
   - Otherwise the engine compares the trend only with your own recent trend, warning on acceleration, a wrong direction and stagnation, exactly as the canonical phase record asks.
   - A trend without that authority is described, never praised or faulted.

Only Weekly is wired to prose. The section contracts, allocation and audits are shared, and they are exercised for every briefing type.

## 6. Weight-rate policy authority audit

**Question.** Were `build_lean_mass` `typicalWeeklyRate [0.25, 1.0]` and `cautionWeeklyRate 1.5` lb/week (A) canonical Goal Contract or product policy, (B) an engine heuristic from this candidate, or (C) derivable from the active Phase or Energy Strategy?

**Answer: B, an engine heuristic.** The thresholds were introduced on this branch (`f02b0340`, re-added `c26c5f72`) and existed nowhere before it. They also **contradicted the canonical record**:

- **The active Phase Expected Trajectory has no rate.** The accepted record in production (`phase_expected_trajectory_v1`, active goal) carries `weightTrajectory: { direction: "goal_and_guardrail_aware", universalWeeklyRate: null }`. The same shape is created in `PhaseEstablishmentService.js:85`.
- **The Phase 2 package declines a rate on purpose.** `FounderPhase2ActivationPackageService.js:211-216` sets `weightTrajectory: { direction: "deliberate_controlled_upward_movement", universalWeeklyRate: null, accelerationWarning, stagnationWarning, volatilityWarning }`. `docs/PHASE_2_ACTIVATION_PACKAGE.md` says: "Weight logic warns on acceleration, stagnation and volatility without inventing a universal weekly rate."
- **Not derivable (C).**
  - No build-phase Energy Strategy carries a weight rate. `executionTargets` holds only calories and activity.
  - The only numeric weight rate anywhere is the Cut strategy's `weeklyLossRange`, calculated from the calorie deficit. That is exactly the calorie-to-weight conversion the handoff forbids.
  - The accepted guardrail "Keep weight gain gradual" has no number.

**What the engine does now**, in `GoalEvidencePolicies.WEIGHT_PACE_AUTHORITY` and `BriefingEvidencePicture`:

1. **Goal types own direction only** (up for lean-mass/weight gain, down for fat loss, stable for maintenance). Weight is always assessed and is never presented as composition.
2. **A canonical expected range, if one exists, is the only thing that can call a pace on-pace, quick or fast.** The range is a signed lb/week `expectedWeeklyRange` (optionally with `cautionWeeklyRate`) on the accepted trajectory's `weightTrajectory`. The consumer is ready but **not yet wired** into the Weekly prepare path. Because every accepted record today says `universalWeeklyRate: null`, production behavior would be identical either way.
3. **Without a canonical range, the trend is judged only against your own trend:**
   - a Theil–Sen pace over the last 28 days;
   - acceleration, meaning the last two weeks' pace is above the two weeks before by more than both a minimum and the noise in the two paces;
   - wrong direction and stagnation relative to the goal's direction, and noise.
   - A trend in the goal's direction is **described, not endorsed**. The polarity is neutral: no "on track", no "too fast".
4. **Measurement boundaries** (0.25 lb/week as "held steady"; acceleration minimums) are owned by the engine. They are labeled in code as measurement, not targets.

**Effect on Sep 20–26.**
- The robust 4-week pace is 1.68 lb/week. The last two weeks ran 2.04 lb/week against 2.59 for the two weeks before, so the trend is not accelerating.
- The engine therefore describes it ("rising about 1.7 lb a week over the last four weeks, in the direction the goal wants") and watches the weekly average. It no longer calls it "climbing fast"; that verdict came entirely from the unowned 1.5 heuristic.
- **This is a policy result, not tuning.** No threshold was changed to make the week read better.

**Founder decision.** If you want the engine to judge the pace of a lean-mass phase in absolute terms (for example, "faster than planned"), that expectation belongs on the accepted Phase Expected Trajectory as a signed `expectedWeeklyRange` (plus an optional caution). It would then need the follow-on wiring of that record into the Weekly prepare inputs. Without it, the engine stays personal-trend-relative, as the canonical record asks.
